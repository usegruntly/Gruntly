# Related work, read 2026-11-11, and what it adds to the plan

Before implementing `docs/plan-2026-11-11.md`, a pass over papers and projects that split work between an expensive and a
cheap model, verify cheap work, or measure agent cost honestly. Numbers are quoted as the sources give them; where a number
comes from a secondary summary rather than the paper, it says so. Nothing here was run.

## 2. Source notes

### Cheap-first cascades and routing
- **AutoMix** (Aggarwal, Madaan et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2310.12963): small model first, few-shot self-verification, a meta-verifier, a POMDP router to the large model; "reducing computational cost by over 50% for comparable performance" (v1 measured a different quantity: up to 89% better incremental benefit per cost). gruntly's gates are a deterministic verifier in the same slot.
- **RouteLLM** (Ong et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2406.18665): routers trained on preference data; cost cut versus GPT-4-only 85% on MT-Bench, 45% MMLU, 35% GSM8K at 95% of GPT-4 quality; routers transfer to model pairs unseen in training. For gruntly the routing question is "delegate or not"; `gruntly stats` already keeps per-harness pass rates, the raw material for a rule.
- **EcoAssistant** (Zhang et al., ICLR 2024 workshop, arXiv 2310.03046): hierarchy of assistants, cheapest first, restart with the next on failure; successful query→code pairs retrieved as demonstrations. Mixed-100: 80% at cost 5.90 vs GPT-4 59% at 13.77.
- **AI Agents That Matter** (Kapoor et al., arXiv 2407.01502): accuracy-only leaderboards mislead; retry, warming and escalation baselines match HumanEval agents at a fraction of the cost (secondary: warming 91% = Reflexion at ~30% less; escalation 86.6% at under half of LDB's cost; LATS over 50x the cost); joint optimisation of cost and accuracy; reproducibility problems in WebArena and HumanEval evaluations.

### Repeated sampling and selection
- **Large Language Monkeys** (Brown et al., arXiv 2407.21787): coverage scales log-linearly with samples; SWE-bench Lite 15.9% → 56% at 250 samples (single-sample SOTA 43%); five DeepSeek samples beat one Claude/GPT sample at over 3x lower cost; with unit tests the coverage gain becomes task performance; majority voting and reward models plateau past ~100 samples.
- **CodeMonkeys** (Ehrlich et al., arXiv 2501.14723): parallel trajectories, each writes a test with its edit; selection by test votes then a model; 57.4% Verified for about $2,300 with Claude 3.5 Sonnet; context identification paid once and shared across samples; selection closes about half the gap between random (45.8%) and oracle (69.8%).
- **S\*** (Li et al., Findings of EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2502.14382): N parallel samples, up to R revision rounds on public tests, selection by adaptively generated distinguishing inputs; GPT-4o-mini + S* beats o1-preview by 3.7% on LiveCodeBench; a 3B model beats GPT-4o-mini.
- **Agentless** (Xia et al., FSE 2025, arXiv 2407.01489): localisation → repair (several candidate patches) → validation (syntax, regression tests, reproduction tests), about $0.34 per issue at 27.33% on SWE-bench Lite (secondary figures), against agent scaffolds at several dollars.
- **Scaling Flaws of Verifier-Guided Search** (Yu et al., ICLR 2025 workshop, arXiv 2502.00271): with imperfect verifiers, verifier-guided search beats plain repeated sampling only at small N; the verifier prunes valid paths, worse on hard and out-of-distribution problems. For gruntly: select with real tests and the parent's holdout, keep N small, never select on model-written tests alone.
- **Cost-of-Pass** (Erol et al., arXiv 2504.13359): expected cost of a correct solution; with paid models, majority voting and self-refinement rarely pay. With a free child, N samples cost wall time and rate limits, not dollars: the one case where sampling is cheap.

### Tests as verifiers
- **UTGen / UTDebug** (Prasad et al., COLM 2025, arXiv 2502.01619): LLM self-evaluation of code is unreliable; a trained generator of failing tests improves debugging (+3.17% HumanEvalFix, +12.35% MBPP+ with Qwen2.5-32B; +13.8% for GPT-4o); validating and back-tracking edits on several generated tests avoids overfitting to one wrong test; UTGen beats an 8B reward model as a judge by 4.43% on HumanEval+ best-of-10.
- **AgentCoder** (Huang et al., arXiv 2312.13010): programmer, test designer and test executor as separate agents; separate test design matters in the ablation; GPT-4 test accuracy 89.6% (HumanEval) and 91.4% (MBPP).

### Cost-controlled evaluation
- **HAL** (Kapoor et al., ICLR 2026, arXiv 2510.11977): 21,730 rollouts, 9 models × 9 benchmarks, ~$40,000; cost-accuracy Pareto frontiers; the most costly models are rarely on the frontier; scaffolds change accuracy and cost drastically.
- **SWE-Effi** (arXiv 2509.09853): resolve rate, CPU time, LLM calls, input/output tokens, normalised inference time; effectiveness = AUC of resolved count against consumption under caps (EuTB 2M tokens, EuCB $1, 30 min); 5 scaffolds × 3 open models on 50 Verified issues; the model matters more than the scaffold (SWE-Agent: Qwen3-32B 28% with 35.5 calls and 440k input tokens, GPT-4o-mini 10% with 181 calls and 8,143k); input tokens dominate output by two to three orders of magnitude in their tables; failed runs burn several times the tokens of successes (secondary: 8.8M vs 1.8M).

### Context cost
- **Anthropic, context management** (anthropic.com/news/context-management): context editing clears stale tool results; −84% tokens in a 100-turn web-search evaluation; memory tool + editing +39% over baseline. Claude Code's compaction is the session-level analogue; the user's lever remains `/compact` and `/clear`.
- **ACON** (Kang et al., arXiv 2510.00615, github.com/microsoft/acon): natural-language compression guidelines refined from failures; 26–54% peak-token reduction; smaller models gain up to 46% as agents.
- **ReWOO** (Xu et al., arXiv 2305.18323): plan every tool call before any observation; about 64% fewer tokens and +4% on HotpotQA (other summaries say 5x). The parent writing one complete request is this shape.
- **RTK** (github.com/rtk-ai/rtk): Bash-only command rewriting hook for Claude Code; claims 60–90% fewer tokens from tool output; built-in Read/Grep/Glob are not covered.
- **Anthropic, multi-agent research system** (anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system): agents use about 4x the tokens of chat, multi-agent systems about 15x; Opus 4 lead + Sonnet 4 subagents +90.2% over single Opus 4 on their internal eval. Delegation to a free child is the same "spend more tokens" lever with the spend moved off the meter.

### Speculative and draft-verify agents
- **Draft-Model Gate** (arXiv 2609.05274): Qwen3-4B teacher-forces over the agent's output, 50 surprisal/entropy features, a linear calibrator vetoes low-probability actions and triggers a replan; SWE-bench Verified execution errors 21% → 15%, tokens −14%, success 49% → 44%; DA-Code errors 14% → 6%, tokens −19%, success 64% → 63%; single runs.
- **Dynamic Speculative Agent Planning** (arXiv 2509.01920): approximation agent proposes k steps, target agent verifies in parallel and wins on mismatch; −30% total cost, up to −60% "unnecessary" cost; saves latency and tokens inside one agent's loop (gruntly's supervised plan review is this at task scale; pipelining steps would save wall time only).
- **MinionS** (Narayan et al., ICML 2025, arXiv 2502.15964): the naive back-and-forth protocol cut remote cost 30.4x but kept only 87% of quality (the local model fails multi-step instructions); decomposition into small parallel chunks with a filtered return cut cost 5.7x at 97.9% (8B local: 97.9% at 18% of cloud cost; 3B: 93.4% at 16.6%). Supports small steps and one filtered block, against chatty `--continue` loops.

### Products that split work the same way
- **aider architect/editor** (aider.chat/2024/09/26/architect.html): numbers in table row 5; the editor "can be assigned to an appropriate model based on cost, speed and code editing skill".
- **Roo Code Orchestrator / Boomerang** (docs.roocode.com/features/boomerang-tasks): subtasks via `new_task`, each mode with its own model, only the completion summary returns to the parent. **Kilo Code** deprecated its Orchestrator (v7.1.10) because its agents gained native subagents: orchestration as a mode gets absorbed into the harness, which is also what atomic-agent's Fusion does. gruntly's defensible parts are the gates, the holdout, the accounting and the local child, not the orchestration.
- **oh-my-opencode** (github.com/code-yeongyu/oh-my-opencode): the Sisyphus orchestrator sends codebase-mapping tasks to cheaper models in the background to keep its context lean; its docs say other models than Claude Opus degrade it significantly. Consistent with not building a scout in gruntly.
- **OpenCode agents** (opencode.ai/docs/agents): per-agent `model` in `opencode.json`; subagents inherit the primary's model unless set; a cheap model can own Explore. A possible child-side setting for gruntly's OpenCode adapter, nothing more.
- **claude-code-router** (github.com/musistudio/claude-code-router): routes Claude Code's `background`, `think` and `longContext` requests to other providers, including a local model for background work; maintenance status unclear. Outside gruntly's scope; worth knowing that Claude Code's own background calls can be moved off the meter.
- **zen-mcp-server**: Claude consults other models (Ollama among them) through MCP with conversation continuity; a consultation pattern, not a delegation with gates.

### Failure taxonomies
- **MAST, Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?** (Cemri et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2503.13657; 1,642 traces): step repetition 15.7%, reasoning-action mismatch 13.2%, unaware of termination conditions 12.4%, disobey task specification 11.8%, incorrect verification 9.1%, no or incomplete verification 8.2%, task derailment 7.4%, fail to ask for clarification 6.8%, premature termination 6.2%. Interventions: better verification prompts gave at most about +5 points (MathChat 84.75 → 89.75; a topology change 85.50, p = 0.4); ChatDev 25.0 → 34.4 (prompt) and 40.6 (topology); verifiers "only superficial checks, despite being prompted to perform thorough verification", "not a silver bullet". Mapping to gruntly: step repetition → loop detector (built); reasoning-action mismatch → narrated-work fail-fast (plan 1.4); termination → budgets (built, sized in 3.2); task specification → scope gate + reviewer; incorrect/no verification → gates decide, child tests unproven (I3, 1.3); premature termination → empty-diff path; fail to ask for clarification → reviewer's "narrowest reading" rule, plus a cheap flag when the child's report says it assumed or was unsure. The intervention numbers are the argument for gates over prompts.

## 3. What this reading does not change
- No several children: no best-of-N sampling, no escalation chain, no second model. One local child on one laptop is the
  product; the sampling literature's gains come from parallel hosted samples and do not transfer to that machine.
- No scout or read-only delegation: ACON, oh-my-opencode and Minions all work on the reading side, but Claude Code's own sub-agents already do that and the measured context growth is mostly the parent's own output, not tool results.
- No Haiku child harness and no atomic-agent adapter (decided before this pass; nothing here reopens them).
- Harness-internal techniques (grammar-locked calls, KV-cache pinning, result aging, compaction) stay inside the harness.
- The two-turn shape stands: every source that measured cost found the expensive model's turns and context, not the cheap model's tokens, to be the bill.
